ScrollScout — a diagnostic for ink detection on unread scrolls
github.com/FrankTheRope/scrollscout · MIT · tag v0.8

ScrollScout answers one question about an ink prediction: does this look like writing? It measures the prominence of line periodicity in 10 mm windows, gates it on plausible ink coverage, and ranks the windows so that a human looks at the most writing-like places first. CPU-only, no training, seconds per segment. It ranks; it does not decide. Every window it flagged in the work below was looked at by eye, and the images are in the docs.

What it establishes, all reproducible from the repo:
• Separation with no overlap. On real predictions, text-bearing segments score 1.000 and raw papyrus 0.11–0.26 at the 95th percentile. Raw ink fraction — the trivial baseline — is 1.000 on both classes and separates nothing.
• Feature selection by measurement. Four hand-designed sub-scores were reduced to one by experiment: ink_fraction takes a single value across a segment (demoted to a gate), stroke_shape inverts sign on real data (no vote), anisotropy changes AP by +0.001 on six annotated PHerc0139 segments (dropped). Each removal is documented with the numbers that forced it.
• Cross-model concordance as a command: on w035, ink_9um (9 µm) and canon_2um (2.4 µm) rankings agree at 0.64; two seeds agree at 0.78 — the ranking follows the papyrus, not the model.
• A map of every public segment of the eligible scrolls that have any, run through the public models and ranked. PHerc1447: the 3 pre-rendered segments, the 11 unrendered meshes rendered locally, and the 22 raw z_dbg_gen segments with the team's gp/s5/tracer_ft previews (four model families, 25+22 segments, three depth windows where we ran the models ourselves). PHerc0800: all 6 meshes. PHerc1203: all 22 meshes, rendered at the model's native 9.362 µm. About 1 400 predictions, roughly 100 000 windows. Not one window where independent models agree on writing-like structure and the eye confirms it. Every above-threshold window is documented with the models side by side, so anyone can disagree with the eye.
• Rendering without the full VC3D build. vc_render_tifxyz compiles alone, without Ceres; the local render matches the official surface volume at 0.998. Recipe in RENDERING.md, Kaggle inference script in docs/experiments/ (two seeds on two T4s; batch size 16 is the difference between 25 % and full GPU use).
• Three false-positive modes named and characterised, two of them found this month: line periodicity saturates at 1.000 on broad bands at the right pitch even with nothing letter-like in them; and scoring at ±45° to catch tilted writing aligns the fibre bundles with the detector — on PHerc1203 every window two seeds agreed on sat at −45°. Seed agreement is not evidence against papyrus structure, because both seeds see the same fibres.
• A limitation found and kept: within a fully written segment, the tool is not shown to beat raw ink fraction (p = 0.56), and the run that showed it also showed why — the ink_9um labels mark seven letters on a segment full of text. Documented in benchmark_pherc0139.md.

What it does not do: it does not read, and it cannot tell "no ink" from "no ink these models can see". On PHerc1447's raw segments the above-threshold windows are row-like band patterns at 3.5–4.5 mm pitch that weak models might produce on real text; they are set aside for human review, not dismissed, and shared with the segmentation team.
