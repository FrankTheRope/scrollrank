ScrollScout, September 2026: the eligible-scroll map, a positive control on the official ink labels, and a negative test of geometry supervision
https://github.com/FrankTheRope/scrollscout/tree/progress-2026-09b · MIT

In short: ScrollScout ranks windows of an ink prediction (10 mm in this work) by how much they look like writing (line periodicity, gated on plausible ink coverage), on CPU, seconds per segment, so a person looks at the most writing-like places first. It ranks; it does not read. This month I ranked ink predictions for every public segment of the Grand-Prize-eligible scrolls but one (three are too small for a 10 mm window); checked on the official ink labels of a read scroll (PHerc1667) what such a negative is worth; and tested a new idea for ink detection on unread scrolls, which failed on the one crop tried, with a likely mechanism measured.

The form's four questions, briefly. (1) Scroll data: PHerc1447, PHerc1203 and PHerc0800 (every public segment but one); PHerc1667 w028 with the public ink-labels 2026-07; PHerc0139 w035 as the text reference. (2) How it helps reading: it shows on real data where the public ink model stands on the eligible scrolls (nothing writing-like anywhere) and how to read that: on known text on another scroll the same model finds the letters only as blobs, so these negatives cannot be read as "no ink". It reports a clean negative, on one crop, for one tempting shortcut (writing geometry as the only supervision). And it flags two traps that silently corrupt results: local renders that come out in reversed depth order, and gains measured against another model's prediction. (3) New: ink_9um predictions of all 22 PHerc1203 meshes, a scroll with no published ink prediction (scores and previews in the docs; the prediction files are not redistributed and regenerate from the scripts); the translation between the official w028 label canvas and the Data Browser surface volume, so maps of my w028 crop can be scored on the official labels; an importable ink_9um inference module and a guarded fine-tuning harness for the public checkpoint, both run on a free Kaggle T4. (4) Evidence: docs/results.md indexes one page per experiment, each with its data paths and the script behind its numbers (the PHerc0800 table has no script yet); figures in docs/img; the repository is pinned at tag progress-2026-09b.

1. The eligible-scroll map. My own ink_9um runs (two seeds, both depth orders; three depth windows except on the pre-rendered PHerc1447 segments 235910 and 034159, default window only) on 14 of PHerc1447's 15 named segments (11 rendered locally), on all 22 PHerc1203 meshes at the model's native 9.362 µm, and on PHerc0800's 6 segments (5 large enough to score, default depth only); plus a screening of the team's gp/s5/tracer_ft predictions on 22 raw PHerc1447 meshes. Over 550 predictions, about 100 000 window scorings (each window position scored once per prediction and rotation). No window where independent runs agree on writing-like structure and the eye confirms it; the strongest candidates are shown with the runs side by side. Not yet run: the PHerc1447 z_dbg_gen_00320 surface volume. Lesson: rotating ±45° to catch tilted writing also aligns fibre bundles with the detector (on PHerc1203 every window both seeds supported sat at the fibre angle).

2. A positive control on the public ink-labels (2026-07). On a text-dense 27 × 19 mm crop of PHerc1667 w028 (2.4 µm volume resampled to 9.6 µm), public ink_9um (one seed, central depth window) reaches pixel AUC 0.886 against the official labels in their validation region, which lies in the half of the crop kept out of all training (the label offset was fitted on this map; 8 px away it gives 0.880). The team's published prediction of w028 ("canon" below) reaches 0.930. ink_9um draws blobs where the letters are, not glyphs, and with the same slices in reversed depth order it falls to 0.689. So the eligible-scroll negatives cannot be read as "there is no ink".

3. Writing geometry as the only supervision: negative on this crop. Interline gaps as certain negatives, row bands as positive bags. Trained from scratch on 2.6 cm²: AUC 0.51 on the official labels, i.e. chance. Used to fine-tune ink_9um: worse in all four configurations (row bands from ink_9um or from canon, with or without distillation): 0.800–0.845 against 0.886. Likely mechanism, measured against canon because the training half has no official labels: canon marks 13.6 % of the truth-free arm's "certain negative" pixels as ink (ascenders, descenders, rows off the profile; canon over-marks ink, so the true share is lower), and in the truth-free arms the AUC loss is two to three times larger outside the row bands than inside. One seed per configuration; more training area was not tried.

4. Two method lessons. (a) A gain scored against another model's prediction is not a gain in ink detection: trained on canon, ink_9um gained 0.020 against canon on the held-out half, but on the labelled pixels it gained 0.006 against canon and nothing on the official labels (−0.015, block-bootstrap 95 % interval −0.032 to +0.005). (b) A truth-free guard that the loss optimises is blind: with self-distillation on, the guard measured the very agreement the distillation enforced, and stayed green for all 400 steps while the model lost 0.041 AUC on the official labels (0.886 → 0.845). With distillation off the guard fired and rolled back, yet the resumed run ended as the worst of the fine-tuned models (0.800).

5. Reusable pieces (scripts in docs/experiments/). ink9um_api.py: villa's ink_9um inference as an importable module (villa ships it as a command-line script; correlation 0.99998 with its output). mil_finetune_ink9um.py: a fine-tuning harness for the public checkpoint with trust region, L2-SP, distillation and bootstrap-calibrated guards with rollback. eval_official_labels_1667.py: the label-canvas offset and the scoring of maps of the w028 crop. mechanism_finetune_1667.py: the loss analysis above. vc_render_tifxyz built without Ceres, after a pointer from Paul (VC team), in docs/RENDERING.md; warning: the local render stack comes out in reversed depth order relative to the official surface volume. Free-Kaggle recipes: kaggle_ink9um_sweep.sh, KAGGLE_mil_finetune.md.

Corrections. Two claims withdrawn, with dated notes in the docs. villa's "reverse" direction is the same slices in reversed depth order, not the other face of the sheet, so my reading of it as a written-side test is withdrawn (I re-checked villa's code after a Discord remark from Sean of the segmentation team). And my claim that "13 cm² of true labels" improve ink_9um is withdrawn: those labels were the canon prediction, and the area was 2.1 cm². Also corrected before this submission: the from-scratch training area (2.6 cm², not 13), the render check (0.998 holds for the central slice only) and PHerc1447's coverage (14 of 15 named segments).

Limits. One seed per configuration, one crop; every official-label number rests on 0.29 cm² (a 9.6 × 3.6 mm strip, about one and a half letters, 11 % of the held-out half).
