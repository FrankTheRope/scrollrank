ScrollScout, September 2026: the eligible-scroll map, a positive control on the official ink labels, and a negative test of geometry supervision
https://github.com/FrankTheRope/scrollscout/tree/progress-2026-09 · MIT

In short: ScrollScout ranks 10 mm windows of an ink prediction by how much they look like writing (line periodicity, gated on plausible ink coverage), on CPU, seconds per segment, so a person looks at the most writing-like places first. It ranks; it does not read. This month I used it to run and rank every public segment of the Grand-Prize-eligible scrolls but one, measured on the official ink labels why nothing showed up, and tested a new idea for ink detection on unread scrolls: it failed, for a measured reason.

1. The eligible-scroll map. My own ink_9um runs (two seeds, three depth windows, both depth orders) on 14 of PHerc1447's 15 named segments (11 rendered locally), on all 22 PHerc1203 meshes at the model's native 9.362 µm (no ink prediction of this scroll was public before), and on PHerc0800's 6 segments (5 large enough to score, default depth only); plus a screening of the team's gp/s5/tracer_ft predictions on 22 raw PHerc1447 meshes. Over 550 predictions, about 100 000 scored windows. No window where independent runs agree on writing-like structure and the eye confirms it; the strongest candidates are shown with the runs side by side. Not yet run: the PHerc1447 z_dbg_gen_00320 surface volume. Lesson: rotating ±45° to catch tilted writing also aligns fibre bundles with the detector (on PHerc1203 every seed-supported window sat at the fibre angle).

2. A positive control on the public ink-labels (2026-07). On a text-dense crop of PHerc1667 w028 (2.4 µm volume resampled to 9.6 µm), public ink_9um (one seed, central depth window) reaches pixel AUC 0.886 against the official labels in their validation region, which lies in my held-out half; the team's canon prediction reaches 0.930. It draws blobs where the letters are, not glyphs, and with the same slices in reversed depth order it falls to 0.689. So the eligible-scroll negatives mean "the model sees dimly", not "there is no ink".

3. Writing geometry as the only supervision: negative on this crop. Interline gaps as certain negatives, row bands as positive bags. Trained from scratch on 2.6 cm²: AUC 0.51 on the official labels, i.e. chance. Used to fine-tune ink_9um: worse in all four configurations (0.800–0.845 against 0.886). The cause, measured against the canon prediction because the training half has no official labels: 13.6 % of the "certain negative" pixels are ink (ascenders, descenders, rows off the profile), and the AUC loss is largest in the interlines. More training area was not tried.

4. Two method lessons. (a) Fine-tuning toward another model's predictions moves toward that model, not toward the ink: trained on the canon prediction, ink_9um gained 0.020 against canon and lost 0.015 against the official labels. (b) A truth-free guard that the loss optimises is blind: with self-distillation on, the guard measured the very agreement the distillation enforced, and stayed green while a truth diagnostic fell from 0.58 to 0.51. With distillation off the guard fired and rolled back, yet the resumed run ended as the worst model of the study.

5. Reusable pieces. ink9um_api.py: villa's ink_9um inference as a small module (correlation 0.99998 with villa's own output). mil_finetune_ink9um.py: a fine-tuning harness for the public checkpoint with trust region, L2-SP, distillation and bootstrap-calibrated guards with rollback. eval_official_labels_1667.py: aligns the w028 label canvas to the Data Browser surface volume (a translation) and scores any map on it. vc_render_tifxyz built without Ceres, after a pointer from Paul (VC team); warning: the local render stack comes out in reversed depth order relative to the official surface volume. Free-Kaggle recipes.

Corrections, dated in the docs. villa's "reverse" direction is the same slices in reversed depth order, not the other face of the sheet; my earlier reading of it as a written-side test is withdrawn. So is my earlier claim that "13 cm² of true labels" improve ink_9um: those labels were the canon prediction, and the area was 2.1 cm².

Limits. One seed per configuration, one crop; the official labels cover 11 % of the held-out half.
